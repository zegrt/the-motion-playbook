# Core

Read this before any motion work. Seven principles, each with the reason it matters. The chapters hold the rules that come
from them; each rule names its principle.

**1. Know what each moment is for.**
Every shot has one job in the whole piece and every frame has one main read. Decide that before building anything, and let
pose and timing carry the message before effects and detail do. Stepping back means asking this question again: what is
this shot for, and how does it sit between the shots around it.

**2. Judge what the viewer sees.**
The screen is the truth, not the settings. Measure speed, size, blur and angle where they land on screen, inside the time
the thing is actually visible. It cuts both ways: what the viewer can't see is not a fault, and work nobody will see is
wasted.

**3. Caused and continuous.**
Something leads every change, and everything answers it in a chain. Parts overlap instead of taking turns, things are
already moving when we cut to them, and nothing moves in lockstep. Motion that stops and starts, or waits for the previous
action to finish, reads as mechanical.

**4. Keep the meaning through the motion.**
Every in-between frame still has to say the right thing. Motion can turn a plus into a times sign, a number into a wrong
number, a tag into a sum. Check the whole frame at speed, not only the poses.

**5. A living frame, with restraint.**
Everything on screen has some life from its first visible frame, with intention behind it. Secondary motion, ambient
motion and effects are planned with the main action, not added at the end. Life is not clutter: one accent, one main read,
the rest quieter.

**6. One source of truth.**
One camera per shot, one true size per object, one owner per animated property, one master per asset, one record per
production. Copies drift; derive everything else from the one source.

**7. Change the least that fixes it.**
When revising, fix the flagged thing on the latest approved version and leave the rest alone. Fix the cause (camera,
light, timing) rather than painting over it with detail or glow. When exploring, go wide; the restraint is for revisions.

# Story and edit

The whole piece before the parts: through-line, beats, what each shot is for, read time, cuts, transitions, timing to the
voice, the whole at speed, and versions. Tool-free.

Principles behind this chapter: [1 Know what each moment is for](../CORE.md), [2 Judge what the viewer sees](../CORE.md),
[3 Caused and continuous](../CORE.md).

## The whole and its beats

### A through-line, even in a feature film
`id: story.through-line` · principle 1

- **Rule.** Even a piece that mostly shows features needs a rough narrative to follow from start to end: one thread the
shots hang on.
- **Why.** Without a through-line, a feature film is a list, and lists don't hold attention.
- **Basis.** principle; director notes. **Scope.** any piece longer than a few seconds.

### Agree the beats before building
`id: story.beats-first` · principle 1

- **Rule.** Before building a shot, agree in words what it shows and what its beat is. Then act every element, including
the character, to that beat. Don't invent sequences the story doesn't need.
- **Why.** A shot built before its beat is agreed gets rebuilt; acting without a beat is busy, not expressive.
- **Basis.** director notes. **Scope.** any shot.

### Every shot does something different
`id: story.distinct-shots` · principle 1

- **Rule.** Check neighbouring shots against each other: each one has to move the story, the information or the feeling
somewhere new.
- **Why.** Shots refined one at a time can drift until several do the same job, and then none of them lands.
- **Basis.** director notes. **Scope.** any sequence.
- **Check.** Name each shot's job in five words. Two identical names means one shot should go or change.

### Each shot brings its own elements
`id: story.own-elements` · principle 1

- **Rule.** A shot's background and secondary elements come from what that shot is about; pieces that belong to another
shot stay out. Things glimpsed in an overview or a montage are previews of the rest of the film, not set dressing to
carry into later shots.
- **Why.** Carried into a shot that isn't about them, they crowd it with things that aren't its subject and blur what
the shot is for.
- **Basis.** director notes. **Scope.** any piece with an overview and feature scenes.

## Time on screen

### Find the time the audience really has
`id: story.visible-window` · principles 1, 2

- **Rule.** Before planning a shot's beats, measure when titles, characters or crops cover it. Put the beats in the visible
window; leave only drift and light behind the cover.
- **Why.** In a 4.5 second shot whose title lands at 2.0 seconds and then fills the frame, the world has about 2 seconds to
do its job. A pulse added to every piece was invisible under the title.
- **Basis.** measured. **Scope.** any shot with titles or overlays.

### Give information a read
`id: story.read-time` · principles 1, 4

- **Rule.** In a teaching or information beat, give each idea a held read while the ambient motion carries on. One idea at
a time, centred on its card, and say what numbers are ("AVG 4.2 s → NOW 1.8 s", not "4.2 s" alone).
- **Why.** Information that moves on before it's read is decoration. As a measured example: 0.3 to 0.45 seconds each for a
question, a trick and a result was right; adding 1.6 seconds in total was too slow, and about 0.85 seconds was enough.
- **Basis.** measured; director notes. **Scope.** any information beat.

### Cut when the beat is done
`id: story.cut-earlier` · principle 1

- **Rule.** Once a beat has landed, cut. Trim tails, darks and holds that only wait, and move everything after them up.
A short empty beat (about 0.2 seconds) can be worth keeping when it gives the next moment room; the rest goes.
- **Why.** Most rough cuts hold too long after the point is made: "it doesn't need to be two whole seconds."
- **Basis.** director notes. **Scope.** any cut.

### Retime the beat with its word
`id: story.retime-with-vo` · principles 1, 3

- **Rule.** When retiming a beat, move the voice-over word that belongs to it, so the word stays on the moment it
describes. Land words on picture by measuring where the word starts in the audio
([motion.word-sync](motion-and-timing.md#land-on-the-word-not-the-time)).
- **Why.** A beat moved without its word desynchronises the sentence from the picture.
- **Basis.** measured. **Scope.** any piece with voice-over.

### Cut on motion
`id: story.cut-on-motion` · principle 3

- **Rule.** Cut while things are moving, into shots already in motion
([motion.already-moving](motion-and-timing.md#already-moving-at-the-cut)).
- **Why.** Motion carries the eye across the cut; a cut from stillness to stillness reads as a slideshow.
- **Basis.** principle; director notes. **Scope.** any cut.

### Transitions are moments
`id: story.transitions` · principles 1, 3

- **Rule.** Treat a transition as a beat of its own: it can stack events (light changing, a reaction, things returning)
and it should keep its momentum. Keep it short enough to keep the film's beat.
- **Why.** A transition built as one thing at a time loses the energy that carries the viewer into the next scene.
- **Basis.** director notes. **Scope.** any transition.

### Seamless joins are designed across both shots
`id: story.seamless-join` · principles 3, 6

- **Rule.** For an invisible cut, design the outgoing shot's last frames and the incoming shot's first frames together:
same positions, same speed, same look. Hand over with the outgoing shot's last frame rendered without the element that
continues, rather than masking it out.
- **Why.** A join matched in position but not in speed shows as a hitch; a mask leaves a hole as soon as the element moves.
- **Basis.** measured. **Scope.** any invisible cut.
- **Check.** Track the continuing element's position and speed across the cut, frame by frame, and diff the last outgoing
frame against the first incoming frame numerically.

## The whole at speed

### Review the whole film at speed
`id: story.whole-at-speed` · principles 1, 2

- **Rule.** Before calling anything final, watch the whole piece at full speed, with sound when it exists, start to end,
and note the joins between scenes.
- **Why.** Frame checks find errors but not feel. Shots approved one by one can still fight each other in sequence.
- **Basis.** principle. **Scope.** any piece.
- **Check.** Say in the delivery note whether this was done, and with or without sound.

### Keep earlier versions reachable
`id: story.keep-versions` · principle 7

- **Rule.** Keep earlier versions of a shot or cut reachable while it's being refined.
- **Why.** Notes often land "somewhere between this and the last one", which needs both to hand.
- **Basis.** director notes. **Scope.** any iteration.

## Versions and variants

### A variant keeps the master's timing
`id: story.variant-timing` · principles 6, 7

- **Rule.** Build a variant (a no-price version, a different end card, a language version) from the approved master: copy
its keys and eases, change values, never times. If a move gets shorter, scale its values and ease speeds together.
- **Why.** Variants that keep the master's timing stay in sync with the same sound and the same edit, and stay true
siblings of the approved version.
- **Basis.** measured. **Scope.** any variant.
- **Check.** Diff the variant against the master frame by frame: it should differ only where the content changed. After
shortening a move, check nothing new crosses its path.

### The simplest variant often wins
`id: story.variant-remove` · principle 7

- **Rule.** For a reduced variant, try removing the element first, before redesigning the space around it.
- **Why.** A removal keeps everything approved in place; redesigns of a variant multiply review rounds.
- **Basis.** one strong example. **Scope.** any reduced variant.

### Plan versions with clean edit points
`id: story.edit-points` · principle 1

- **Rule.** When cutdowns or other formats are coming (a 15 second cut, other aspect ratios), plan them with the master:
know where the clean edit points are, and ask the composer to build music with them.
- **Why.** Versions cut from a master with no edit points in mind fight the music and the beats.
- **Basis.** principle. **Scope.** any piece with versions.

# Working agreement

Defaults for how an agent works with any director. The working style of whoever runs the session overrides them where
it says something different ([the template](../profiles/_template.md), working style); where it's silent, or there's
no profile, these hold.

Principles behind this file: [1 Know what each moment is for](../CORE.md), [5 A living frame, with
restraint](../CORE.md), [7 Change the least that fixes it](../CORE.md).

### Protect the work instead of asking
`id: work.protect` · principle 7

- **Rule.** Standing steps in the production's own process (claiming a tool, logging, its checks) need no permission.
Protecting the work replaces asking: check for unsaved changes and snapshot them, then work in a new versioned file.
Stop to ask only when real work would be lost.
- **Why.** Asking before a standing step costs the director a turn for nothing. The real risk is losing their unsaved
work, and the check prevents that.
- **Basis.** taste (one director, one case). **Scope.** any session in a tool someone else also works in.
- **In tools.** `ae.rebuild-once` (saving before a restart).

### A yes covers only what it answered
`id: work.yes-scope` · principle 7

- **Rule.** An approval covers only the proposal it answered. When the director gives a new number or direction
mid-build, stop and show the revised plan before any edit. After a rejected look, bring a plan and a cheap test
(mockups or stills, `verify.test-small`) before building again.
- **Why.** A retime rewritten straight from a new number had to be stopped mid-build, and a rejected layout went
straight into fitting scripts instead of a cheap test. Both cost a build the director then threw away.
- **Basis.** taste (one director, two cases). **Scope.** any revision.

### Plan each shot in one block
`id: work.shot-plan` · principles 1, 5

- **Rule.** A plan gives each shot its own block: staging; the main action with its fundamentals in frames
(anticipation, arcs, ease, contacts, squash, follow-through and overlap); acting, if there's a character; secondary
motion; ambient motion, alive from the first visible frame; effects; and the checks it must pass. Then one scene-wide
list of ambient motion and the rules that apply. After a redesign, write the block again (`motion.living-frame`,
Check).
- **Why.** Plans without fixed parts kept dropping secondary motion, ambient motion and effects: the director asked for
them four times in two days.
- **Basis.** taste (one director, four notes in two days). **Scope.** any plan with shots.
- **Check.** Every shot in the plan has every part, or says why one is empty.
